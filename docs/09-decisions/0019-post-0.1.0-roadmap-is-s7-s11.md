# 0019 — the closed post-0.1.0 roadmap is S7–S11, and provider routing with local runtimes stays a later candidate

**Contract**
- **Role:** one decision, with its context and consequences.
- **Mutability:** frozen once accepted. A reversal is a new record that
  supersedes this one; this file is never rewritten.
- **Read when:** before scheduling a post-0.1.0 capability, or before asking why
  the roadmap counts five milestones.
- **Update when:** never. A reversal is a new record.

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Decided by:** owner — the boundary is the one already expressed in the roadmap plan approved on 2026-10-16; this record states it once and corrects decision 0018
- **Supersedes:** 0018 (its sixth gap only; the boundary itself stands)

## Context

Decision 0018 named six production gaps for the post-0.1.0 roadmap, and put
"provider router / local runtimes" in the sixth. The roadmap plan the owner
approved the same day schedules five milestones, S7 to S11, and lists
"provider router / fallback and local runtimes" among its later candidates
(`docs/plans/2026-10-16-post-0.1.0-roadmap-plan.md:136-137`). The 2026-10-16
handoff entry then called S7–S11 "the whole post-0.1.0 roadmap", which reads as
a claim that 0018's sixth gap had been scheduled with the rest. The 2026-10-21
review recorded the disagreement between the decision and the plan.

Left alone, a reader of 0018 would plan a provider router — fallback policy,
provider selection, local runtime packaging — that no phase contains, and would
then have to explain why the roadmap reported itself done without it. The
reverse error is worse: quietly dropping the sixth gap from the record would
lose the fact that the owner declined to schedule it.

## Decision

The closed post-0.1.0 roadmap is milestones S7–S11: multi-agent orchestration,
durable runs with human-in-the-loop, tracing and evaluation, persistent
sessions, and guardrails with structured output. A provider router / fallback
layer and local model runtimes are not part of it. They stay later candidates
beside the platform gaps, revisited only when a user asks and a seam can host
them without special-casing the loop.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why it was not chosen |
|---|---|
| **A — keep 0018's six gaps and schedule a sixth milestone now** | The owner never scheduled one. The approved plan carries five phases, and inventing a phase in a document is not a correction of the boundary. |
| **B — rewrite 0018 in place** | Its contract freezes it once accepted. The five scheduled gaps and the library-not-platform boundary are unchanged, so a rewrite would damage the part that is still true. |
| **C — record the five-milestone roadmap in a new record that supersedes only 0018's gap list** (chosen) | Keeps the boundary and its reasoning as 0018 wrote them, and leaves exactly one place that says what is scheduled. |

## Consequences

**Positive**

- "The roadmap is complete" becomes a true statement: S7–S11 were the closed
  roadmap and all five landed on 2026-10-16.
- A future request for provider routing or local runtimes starts from "later
  candidate" rather than from "scheduled but missing".

**Negative / accepted costs**

- The project has no provider fallback policy and no local runtime story until
  someone asks; that is a real gap, only an unscheduled one.
- Two records now describe this boundary. A reader who stops at 0018's Decision
  section still sees the sixth gap, though its status line points here.

## Reversibility

Easy. Scheduling either capability is a new record that moves it into the
roadmap; nothing was built on the sixth gap, so no code or published surface
changes.
